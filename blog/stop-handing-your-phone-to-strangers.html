The photo you get back is blurry, tilted, and taken from wherever they were already standing. Here is why that keeps happening.

Stop handing your phone to strangers

The short version

  • A stranger doing you a favour takes one rushed photo from where they were already standing.
  • The place you travelled to ends up as a smudge behind your head.
  • A session is nine photos and a short video, taken by somebody whose only job for those five minutes is the photograph.
  • Send a request in the app instead.

You are somewhere worth remembering. You look around, pick the friendliest face, and hand over your phone.

What comes back is almost never what you wanted. The framing is tight, so the place you travelled to is a smudge behind your head. The focus is on the wall. Somebody blinked. And you cannot ask for another one without feeling rude, so you say thank you and walk away with it.

Why it goes wrong

The stranger is doing you a favour, and favours are rushed. They take one photo, not nine and a video. They stand where they were already standing rather than backing up. They do not know that the fountain behind you is the whole point.

What to do instead

Send a request in the app. A verified local nearby comes over, and for those five minutes the photograph is the only thing they are there to do: far enough back that the place is in the frame with you, and enough shots that one blink does not cost you the picture.

Nine photos and one short video, for $19.99, and you are actually in them.
