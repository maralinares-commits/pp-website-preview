The five minutes that make a good session

The short version

  • Most of what separates a good session from a bad one happens in the first thirty seconds.
  • Introduce yourself and say what is about to happen, so people stop bracing.
  • Step back further than feels right: the place is the reason they asked.
  • One instruction at a time, shoot past nine, and finish by checking they are happy.

A session is short. Most of what separates a good one from a bad one happens in the first thirty seconds.

Say hello properly

A client who feels relaxed takes a better photo. Introduce yourself, say what is about to happen, and they stop bracing.

Step back further than feels right

The reason they asked is the place they are standing in. If the frame is only faces, they could have done that themselves.

Give one instruction at a time

Turn slightly left. Chin down a little. Walk towards me. People cannot process three at once, and they look stiff while they try.

Take more than you need

Nine photos is the delivery, not the target. Shoot past it so they have a choice, and so one blink does not cost you the shot.

Finish clearly

Tell them you are done and check they are happy before you walk away. That is the part they rate.
